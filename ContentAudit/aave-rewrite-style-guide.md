# AAVE Bible Rewrite Style Guide

This guide is for future editorial passes on AAVE scripture text. It uses the tone classifications and rewrite priority list to protect the voice of the translation while reducing phrasing that feels forced, time-sensitive, comedic, or AI-translated.

Do not use this guide to rewrite production JSON automatically. Every edit should be reviewed against source meaning, context, and passage tone.

## Core Rewrite Rules

1. Preserve AAVE grammar and rhythm.
2. Remove time-sensitive slang such as `bruh`, `no cap`, `pull up`, and `wildin`.
3. Maintain sacred tone for God speaking, prophecy, covenant language, judgment, blessing, curse, sin, holiness, and worship.
4. Replace slang with culturally rooted but timeless phrasing.
5. Do not modernize tone beyond what feels generationally durable.

## Editorial North Star

The line should sound natural when read aloud by a Black church elder, parent, teacher, or preacher across generations. If the wording depends on a current social-media phrase to feel AAVE, it is probably too narrow. Keep the cadence, directness, warmth, and familiar syntax; remove the trend-chasing.

## Tone Ladder: Casual To Sacred

| Level | Register | Best use | Avoid |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Casual conversation | Human dialogue, narrative banter, everyday scenes | Using casual jokes in divine speech or judgment |
| 2 | Warm narrative | Story flow, travel, introductions, scene-setting | Making every action sound like slang |
| 3 | Clear teaching | Epistles, Jesus explaining, wisdom instruction | Distracting from doctrine with trendy idioms |
| 4 | Poetic / prayerful | Psalms, songs, lament, worship | Flattening emotional weight into casual phrasing |
| 5 | Sacred / covenantal | God speaking, prophecy, judgment, blessing, curse, covenant | Comedy, memes, social-media vocabulary, exaggerated dialect |

Use the highest tone required by the passage. A verse can be AAVE and still sound sacred.

## Banned Or Strongly Discouraged Word List

These terms should usually be removed from scripture text, especially in `SOLEMN`, `PROPHECY`, `TEACHING`, and `POETIC` passages.

| Avoid | Why | Prefer |
|---|---|---|
| `bruh` / `bro` | Too casual; can make sacred moments sound comedic | `my brother`, `friend`, direct address, or omit |
| `no cap` | Time-sensitive and social-media coded | `for real`, `truly`, `I’m telling the truth`, or omit |
| `pull up` / `pulled up` | Modern idiom; too casual for divine action | `came`, `arrived`, `showed up`, `came near` |
| `wildin` / `wildin’` | Comedic or dismissive in solemn contexts | `acting reckless`, `rebelling`, `living wrong`, `doing evil` |
| `trippin` / `trippin’` | Casual and comedic | `stumbling`, `falling off`, `getting caught up`, `going wrong` |
| `chillin` / `chillin’` | Too relaxed for worship, prophecy, or holiness | `resting`, `dwelling`, `sitting`, `living in peace` |
| `turnt` / `lit` | Party/comedy register | `filled with joy`, `shining`, `full of life`, `burning bright` |
| `lowkey` | Time-sensitive and weakens serious claims | `quietly`, `in secret`, `deep down`, or omit |
| `clout` / `hype` | Platform/social-media language | `praise`, `honor`, `attention`, `reputation`, `excitement` |
| `from the jump` | Useful but over-modernized in doctrine | `from the beginning`, `from the start` |
| `straight up` | Can be useful, but overused as filler | `truly`, `plainly`, `clearly`, or omit |
| `fam` | Warm but too casual in sacred passages | `family`, `brothers and sisters`, `people`, or omit |

## Terms To Use With Restraint

These are not banned, but they should not become the whole texture of the translation.

| Use carefully | Guidance |
|---|---|
| `ain’t` | Natural in AAVE, but high repetition can feel like a crutch. Keep where it sounds organic; reduce clusters. |
| `y’all` | Valuable for plural address. Keep where the plural matters; avoid repeating it three or four times in one verse. |
| `gon’` | Natural future marker, but dense repetition can make solemn passages feel casual. Mix with `will`, `is going to`, or restructure. |
| `folks` | Warm and familiar, but can flatten specific groups like priests, elders, nations, disciples, or prophets. |
| `outta`, `tryna`, `gonna-style contractions` | Use for rhythm, not every sentence. |

## Preferred Replacement Patterns

| If the line says... | Try this kind of move |
|---|---|
| `The Lord gon’...` repeated several times | Use `The Lord will...` for sacred statements, or vary sentence structure. |
| `y’all gon’... y’all gon’... y’all...` | Keep one `y’all` if needed, then use `you`, `your people`, or restructure the sentence. |
| `God pulled up` | Use `God came near`, `the Lord came`, `the Lord appeared`, or `the Lord showed up` depending on context. |
| `folks wildin’` | Use `people rebelling`, `people acting wicked`, `people doing wrong`, or `people living reckless`. |
| `no cap` | Use `truth is`, `truly`, `I’m telling the truth`, or remove the emphasis. |
| `bruh` in a serious scene | Use the person’s name, `my brother`, or remove the address. |

## Before / After Examples

These examples are proposed style demonstrations only. They are not production rewrites.

### SOLEMN: Covenant / Blessing

Before:
> If you do, the Lord gon' bless you just like He promised—y'all gon' be lenders, not borrowers. Y'all gon' rule over nations, but ain't nobody gon' rule over you.

After:
> If you obey, the Lord will bless you just like He promised. You’ll lend to nations and won’t have to borrow. You’ll rule over them, and they won’t rule over you.

Why: Keeps direct AAVE rhythm through contraction and sentence flow, but removes the stacked `gon’ / y’all / ain’t` pattern from covenant language.

### SOLEMN: God’s Covenant Faithfulness

Before:
> Then the Angel of the Lord pulled up from Gilgal to Bochim and said, 'Look, I brought y'all outta Egypt, set y'all up in the land I promised your ancestors, and told y'all straight up—I ain't never breaking my covenant with you.'

After:
> Then the Angel of the Lord came from Gilgal to Bochim and said, “Look, I brought y’all out of Egypt and settled you in the land I promised your ancestors. I told you plainly: I will never break My covenant with you.”

Why: Preserves AAVE address and rhythm, but raises the register for divine covenant speech.

### PROPHECY: Judgment Language

Before:
> "Man, y'all a whole sinful nation. Just heavy with mess. Y'all full of wrongdoers—corrupt kids who wildin' out. Y'all done dipped on the Lord and disrespected the Holy One of Israel. Straight up turned your backs."

After:
> “Y’all are a sinful nation, weighed down with wrong. A people full of evil, children who turned corrupt. You left the Lord, disrespected the Holy One of Israel, and turned your backs on Him.”

Why: Keeps the directness and plural address, but removes comedic slang from prophetic rebuke.

### TEACHING: Spirit / Doctrine

Before:
> "But when the Spirit of truth pull up, He gon' guide you into all truth. He ain't speakin' on His own either—He only sayin' what He hear. And He gon' let y'all know what's comin'."

After:
> “But when the Spirit of truth comes, He will guide you into all truth. He won’t speak on His own; He’ll say what He hears, and He’ll show y’all what’s coming.”

Why: Teaching about the Spirit should be clear and weighty. `comes`, `will`, and `won’t` keep the line readable without over-modernizing it.

### TEACHING: Apostolic Authority

Before:
> That's why I was chosen to be a preacher and apostle—I'm speaking the truth in Christ, no cap—a teacher to the Gentiles in faith and truth.

After:
> That’s why I was chosen to be a preacher and apostle. I’m speaking the truth in Christ, for real—a teacher to the Gentiles in faith and truth.

Why: `For real` is more durable than `no cap`, and the sentence break helps preserve spiritual authority.

### POETIC: Peace / Rest

Before:
> Folks gon' be chillin' under they own vine and fig tree—peaceful, unbothered. Ain't nobody out here scaring folks. The Lord Himself said it, and that's on everything.

After:
> People will rest under their own vine and fig tree, peaceful and unbothered. Nobody will make them afraid. The Lord Himself has spoken.

Why: Prophetic-poetic peace should feel steady and reverent. The rewrite keeps warmth but removes casual filler.

## Tone-Specific Guidance

### SOLEMN

- Raise the register immediately when God is speaking or covenant terms appear.
- Use `will` more often than `gon’` when the sentence declares divine action, blessing, curse, or judgment.
- Avoid jokes, casual address, and platform language.

### PROPHECY

- Preserve urgency, warning, lament, and hope.
- Prefer timeless phrases: `the Lord says`, `the day is coming`, `the people will know`, `I will restore`.
- Do not turn prophetic rebuke into roast language.

### TEACHING

- Clarity comes first.
- Keep AAVE rhythm in sentence shape, contractions, and directness.
- Avoid slang that can distract from doctrine.

### POETIC

- Preserve emotional weight, imagery, and cadence.
- Use warm, vivid language, but avoid casual slang that breaks prayer or worship.
- Let repetition feel intentional, not like filler.

### DIALOGUE / NARRATIVE

- These can carry more everyday speech.
- Still avoid slang that will date quickly.
- Human dialogue can sound casual, but sacred scenes still need restraint.

## Rewrite Checklist

Before accepting a rewrite, ask:

- Did we preserve the original meaning?
- Does the AAVE feel natural without being exaggerated?
- Did we remove time-sensitive slang?
- Does God’s speech sound sacred?
- Does prophecy sound weighty?
- Does teaching stay clear?
- Does the line work across generations?
- Would this still feel respectful read aloud in a church setting?

## Working Rule

If a sentence sounds like it is trying to prove it is AAVE, simplify it. If it sounds clear, warm, rhythmic, and reverent, keep it.
