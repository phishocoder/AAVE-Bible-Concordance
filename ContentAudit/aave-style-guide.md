# AAVE Scripture And Commentary Style Guide

Purpose: keep the AAVE Bible Concordance voice clear, reverent, culturally familiar, and readable without making sacred text feel exaggerated or AI-translated.

## Core Principles

- AAVE should feel natural, not exaggerated.
- Clarity comes before slang density.
- Use culturally familiar syntax without making every sentence sound comedic.
- Preserve spiritual weight in serious passages.
- Commentary can be more expressive than scripture text.
- Avoid making sacred text feel like parody.
- Prefer warmth, rhythm, and clarity over stuffing slang into every verse.

## Scripture Voice

- Keep scripture text more restrained than commentary.
- Use AAVE grammar and rhythm where it improves naturalness, not as decoration.
- Avoid stacking multiple slang markers in one verse unless the source passage is informal dialogue and the meaning stays clear.
- Serious passages about God, holiness, sin, death, judgment, worship, suffering, crucifixion, resurrection, and covenant should carry gravity.
- Favor timeless wording over slang that may date quickly.

## Commentary Voice

- Commentary can sound warmer, more conversational, and more expressive than scripture.
- Humor can help explain, but it should not undercut reverence.
- Platform/social-media terms like `clout`, `hype`, or `receipts` should be used sparingly and only when they clarify the point.
- Emoji should be reviewed carefully in commentary tied to solemn passages.

## Terms To Watch

- High repetition watchlist: `ain't`, `gon'`, `y'all`, `folks`.
- High-risk scripture watchlist: `bro`, `bruh`, `no cap`, `turnt`, `lit`, `lowkey`, `clout`, `hype`, `chillin'`, `from the jump`, `wildin'`, `trippin'`.
- Idioms like `pull up`, `straight up`, and `fam` can work, but repeated use can make different books sound like the same speaker.

## Review Checklist

- Does the verse still sound like scripture, not a sketch or paraphrase meme?
- Is the meaning clearer after the AAVE rendering?
- Are slang terms doing real work, or are they just adding flavor?
- Would this wording still feel respectful if read aloud in church?
- Does dialogue sound different from narration?
- Does commentary explain the passage without overpowering it?
- Are repeated terms clustered so tightly that they distract from the message?

## Recommended First Review Areas

1. Chapters with high flag density in scripture, especially theological openings and passion/judgment passages.
2. Any verse where three or more slang markers appear in one line.
3. Commentary entries that use social-media language around holiness, suffering, sin, or worship.
4. Files where `ain't`, `gon'`, `y'all`, and `folks` appear so often that they become the dominant voice.
5. Malformed or hard-to-parse content files before editorial review tooling depends on them.

## Editorial Rule Of Thumb

If a line sounds like it is trying to prove it is AAVE, soften it. If it sounds clear, warm, and spiritually serious while still carrying familiar rhythm, keep it.
